1. Getting started

The welcome screen

The first time you open MyCareRoster, it says who it's for and offers three ways in:

  • Set Up for Myself, if the care you're organizing is your own.
  • Set Up for My Family, for someone you look after, such as a parent or a child.
  • Look Around a Sample Family, to see how everything works first.

You can show the welcome screen again from Settings, under Getting Started.

Already using MyCareRoster on another device? If your roster is waiting in iCloud, the welcome screen says Your roster is in iCloud. Tap Show My Roster.

Guided setup

Setting up takes three short steps, and you can skip any of them:

  1. A name. Your own name, or the name of the person you look after. It's Me and I Look After Someone Else switch between the two.
  2. Their GP or another provider. Add a Provider lets you choose one from Contacts or create one (see Adding providers), or tap Skip for Now. If you add a GP, MyCareRoster asks whether they're that person's GP, and Yes (for example, Yes, Kate's GP) makes them the primary GP.
  3. Details for forms. Fill In Details opens the private profile (see Private profiles), or tap Not Now.

Setup ends on that person's screen (or their private profile, if you chose Fill In Details), with a Getting started checklist of what's left to try: adding a provider, adding an appointment or pasting a reminder, and filling in their details. Tap a line to do it, or close the checklist when you're done. Finish Later stops setup at any step after the first.

MyCareRoster marks you as the person using the app, so it says "your GP" rather than your name. To change who that is, open a family member's ••• menu and choose This Is Me or This Isn't Me.

The sample family

June, Kate, and Isla are fictional, and show how everything works before you add anyone of your own. Explore as much as you like. Nothing about them is saved or synced, their details can't be changed, and their phone numbers, email addresses, and websites don't call, email, or open anything. Their appointments are samples too, and aren't in Calendar. Their private profiles still ask for Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode, so you can see how that works too.

When you're ready, tap Turn Off Sample Data on the Sample Family card at the top of the roster. You can bring the sample back at any time from Settings, with Explore Sample Family. Your own roster isn't changed, and it returns when you turn the sample off again.

Contacts

Your providers' names, phone numbers, and email addresses come from the Contacts app, so there's one place to keep them current. MyCareRoster asks for Contacts access only when you first add a provider. Your family, teams, notes, and private profiles work without it.

On iOS 18 and later you can share all your contacts with MyCareRoster, or only some. Sharing only your providers works fully, and you can share more later from inside the app (see Adding providers).

Each provider you add also goes into a contacts list named Medical, which MyCareRoster creates if you don't have one, so your providers stay together in Contacts. If you have more than one list named Medical, MyCareRoster shows them as one.

If you turned Contacts access off, the roster says Contacts access is off. Tap Open Settings and allow access for MyCareRoster.

2. Finding your way around

The roster, titled Your care network, is home. It lists your family members, each with how many providers they have, and, once you've added providers, All Providers.

At the top of the roster are Settings and a + (Add) menu with:

  • Add Family Member
  • Paste Reminder, for an appointment reminder when you're not sure which provider it's from (see Pasting a reminder)
  • Refresh, which reads Contacts again. You can also pull down on the roster.

Each family member, care team, and team member has a ••• button with the things you can do to it. You can also touch and hold the row.

All Providers

All Providers lists everyone in your family members' care teams once each, sorted by surname, with their specialty and an icon for their kind of care (see A provider's icon). Search by name, specialty, or practice, and, if you have more than one family member, use Showing to see one person's providers or Everyone's.

On iPhone, tap a family member to open their screen, and tap the back button to return. On iPad, and on the larger display of iPhone Duo, the roster stays beside whatever you open. The selected family member is highlighted, and you can hide the roster with the sidebar button.

How This Works

Help text in the app is kept short. For the full story, How This Works on a provider's Appointments card, and How Syncing Works in Settings, open How MyCareRoster works. It explains syncing between your devices, private profiles (including the quick card and Share Summary), appointments, and pasted reminders.

3. Family members

Add everyone whose care you help with, including yourself.

  • Add: on the roster, tap +, then Add Family Member, and enter a name, such as Alex or Mum.
  • Rename or delete: tap the ••• button beside a family member, or touch and hold their name, then choose Rename or Delete. The same options are in the ••• menu on their own screen, with This Is Me.

To set up another person step by step, choose Show Welcome Screen in Settings, then Set Up for My Family.

Deleting a family member removes their teams from MyCareRoster. Providers and their Contacts cards are not deleted.

A family member's screen shows, from the top:

  • their primary GP, with their credentials, their practice, and a button to call them (see Primary GP)
  • their quick card, only if you've turned it on for them on this device (see The quick card)
  • their private profile (see Private profiles)
  • their appointments, with Add Appointment and Paste Reminder (see A family member's appointments)
  • their providers: everyone in their care teams, once each, with their specialty and an icon for their kind of care. Tap one to open their details.
  • their teams, optional groups such as Heart or Physio, with the number of team members in each

At the top of their screen are a + menu (Add Provider and New Team) and a ••• menu (Share Summary, This Is Me, Rename Family Member, and Delete Family Member).

Removing a provider from someone's roster

To take a provider off one person's screen, tap the ••• button beside them in that person's Providers list (or on the primary GP card), or touch and hold them, and choose Remove from Kate's Roster (or Remove from Your Roster for yourself). You can do the same from the provider's own screen, in its Family Members card.

MyCareRoster shows which of that person's teams the provider leaves, and asks you to confirm. Their notes, role, and specialty are kept, as are other family members' teams. The provider and their contact aren't deleted, and their appointments stay in Calendar. If they were that person's primary GP, that person has no primary GP until you choose another.

4. Adding providers

Providers are contacts you choose: GPs, specialists, clinics, therapists, pharmacies, or anyone else involved in care. Add them from a family member's screen or from one of their care teams.

  1. Open the family member, tap +, then Add Provider. Or open one of their teams and tap Add Team Member.
  2. The first time, MyCareRoster explains what it reads from Contacts, under Your providers live in Contacts. Tap Continue, then choose how much to share when iOS asks.
  3. Tap a provider. Your Medical List comes first, then Your Other Providers (or, if you share only some contacts, Shared with MyCareRoster). Type a name or practice in the search field to find anyone else in Contacts.

From a team, the provider joins that team. From the family member's screen, they go into a team named Providers, which MyCareRoster creates if needed. To put them in another team later, use the ••• button beside them (see Care teams).

Someone who isn't in Contacts yet

Under More Ways to Add:

  • Share a Contact, if you share only some contacts (see If you share only some contacts).
  • Choose in Contacts opens Apple's contact picker.
  • Create Provider Contact makes a new contact in Apple's contact editor.
  • Paste Provider Details starts a new contact from details you've copied, such as a practice webpage or an email signature (see Pasting provider details).

Every provider you add also joins your Medical list in Contacts, and this family member's roster or the team you started from.

Contacts lists belong to one account, such as iCloud or Gmail. A contact stored in a different account from your Medical list can't join it, and MyCareRoster won't copy or duplicate it. It's still added as a provider; only the list step is skipped.

If you share only some contacts

MyCareRoster sees only the contacts you've shared, and searches only those. To share another, tap Share a Contact (first under More Ways to Add), type their name, and tap Share beside them. Typing in the search field also offers contacts you haven't shared yet, under Not Shared Yet. They're added as a provider straight away. To share several at once, use the Settings app, under Apps > MyCareRoster > Contacts.

5. Care teams

Care teams group the providers involved in one part of someone's care, such as Cardiology, Physio, or Rehabilitation. Each family member has their own teams, and the same provider can belong to as many teams as you like. The top of a team's screen shows whose team it is, such as Kate's care team.

  • Create a team: on the family member's screen, tap +, then New Team, and enter a name.
  • Add team members: open the team and tap Add Team Member (see Adding providers).
  • Move, copy, or remove a team member: tap the ••• button beside them, or touch and hold their name. Move to and Copy to list this family member's other teams, then other family members' teams. Remove from Team takes them out of this team only; their notes, role, specialty, and other teams are kept. To take them out of all of this person's teams at once, see Removing a provider from someone's roster.
  • Rename or delete a team: tap the ••• button beside the team, or touch and hold it. On the team's own screen, use its ••• menu.

Deleting a team removes it from MyCareRoster. Providers and their Contacts cards are not deleted.

6. A provider's details

Tap a provider on a family member's screen, in a team, or in All Providers to open their details. At the top are their name, with any credentials (such as MBBS FRACGP) on a smaller line below it, their job title from Contacts, and their practice. Under those, MyCareRoster shows who they're the primary GP for, or their specialty.

  • What the letters mean: tap to see what each credential after their name stands for. It also explains titles such as Professor, and why some surgeons are called Mr or Ms. Credentials are shown as written in Contacts; MyCareRoster doesn't check qualifications.
  • Get in touch: calls first, then messaging apps, emails, and websites, named with the labels in their contact card, such as Call Reception. A mobile number has Message beside its call button. If their card has a WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, or Messenger account, you can open a chat there.
  • Locations: every address in their contact card, and Open in Contacts to see or edit their full card. Changes you make there show in MyCareRoster when you close the card.
  • Appointments: their upcoming and past appointments in Calendar, with Add Appointment and Paste (see Appointments).
  • Notes: type in Add a note. It saves when you tap Done or leave the field, and Undo takes it back. Notes are private to MyCareRoster, and they're never written into Contacts. To change or delete a note, tap its ••• button, or touch and hold it.
  • Manage provider: set their Role to General Practitioner, Specialist, or Unclassified. For a specialist, type their specialty. It saves on its own, with Undo, and is shown under their name throughout the app. The Icon row shows the icon beside them in your lists (see A provider's icon).
  • Family Members: each family member whose roster includes them, with the teams they're in, and Remove from Kate's Roster (see Removing a provider from someone's roster).
  • Other contact details: fax and pager numbers, and anything else in their card that can't be called or opened. MyCareRoster never dials these. Tap the copy button beside one to paste it into a referral form.

A provider's icon

Each provider has an icon for their kind of care, such as general practice, heart, eyes, mental health, or bones, joints, and movement. It's shown beside them on a family member's screen, in their teams, and in All Providers.

MyCareRoster picks the icon automatically: a GP always gets the general practice icon; otherwise it reads their job title from Contacts, then the specialty you typed. If it picks the wrong one, open the provider, and under Manage provider, tap Change on the Icon row. Choose any icon, or Automatic to go back to MyCareRoster's choice. Your choice syncs to your other devices.

7. Primary GP

Each family member can have one primary GP, shown at the top of their screen with a button to call them. The same GP can be primary for several family members.

  1. Make sure the GP is in at least one of the family member's care teams.
  2. Open the GP's details and, under Manage provider, set Role to General Practitioner.
  3. Turn on Primary GP for beside the family member's name. If someone else was their primary GP, the switch shows who it replaces.

During guided setup, adding a GP asks whether they're that person's GP, and Yes does all of this for you.

To stop using a GP as primary, turn the same switch off. Removing them from the family member's roster does this too (see Removing a provider from someone's roster). The call button uses the GP's first phone or mobile number (never a fax or pager) and its label from Contacts, for example Call Reception. If the number has no label, it says Call GP.

8. Appointments

Appointments stay in Apple's Calendar app. MyCareRoster shows them on each family member's screen and each provider's screen, and helps you add and update them, but it doesn't keep a copy of them.

Adding an appointment

  1. On a family member's screen or a provider's screen, tap Add Appointment.
  2. From a family member's screen, MyCareRoster asks Who is the appointment with?, listing their providers with their primary GP first. If you don't know yet, choose No Provider Yet; you can link it to a provider later. From a provider's screen in more than one family member's care teams, choose who it's for instead.
  3. The first time, MyCareRoster asks for access to your calendars, and, if you have more than one, which calendar new appointments go in. You can change this later in Settings, under Appointments.
  4. Choose where it is: one of the provider's addresses, Phone Consultation, Video Consultation, or Somewhere Else to type a place yourself.
  5. Calendar's event editor opens, filled in with the provider's name and specialty (or GP), the location, and, if you have more than one family member, who it's for. Set the date and time, change anything else you like, and tap Add.

MyCareRoster adds a link to the event, in its URL field or, if that's in use, its notes. The link is how MyCareRoster knows who the appointment is for and which provider it's with, if any.

A family member's appointments

Each family member's screen has an Appointments card, below their private profile. It lists their Upcoming and Past appointments with every provider in their roster, and the ones not linked to a provider yet. The next one is marked Next. Each group shows three at first; tap Show All with the number (for example, Show All 5) to see the rest, and Show Fewer to fold them again.

  • With Dr Tom Whitfield under an appointment opens that provider. An appointment without one says No provider yet.
  • To link one to a provider, tap its ••• button and choose Link to Provider, then the provider and where it is. MyCareRoster then offers to link other appointments like it.
  • Unlink from the provider moves an appointment back to No provider yet. Remove MyCareRoster Link removes the link; the event stays in Calendar. If it's upcoming and names one of their providers, it's shown again as a possible match.
  • An upcoming event that names one of their providers but wasn't added through MyCareRoster is shown as a Possible match, with Link to that provider if it's for this person. It's never marked Next.

Listing appointments needs full access to your calendars. Appointments linked with an earlier roster can name a family member who isn't in this one. When only this person's teams include the provider, the card lists them and says so: tap the Update Old Link button (for example, Update 3 Old Links) to write this person into those links in Calendar. Only the links change; dates, locations, and notes stay as they are.

A provider's appointments

A provider's screen lists their Upcoming and Past appointments from the last and next 12 months. Use Show Earlier Appointments or Show Later Appointments to look further. If MyCareRoster hasn't asked for Calendar access yet, tap Show Appointments. If Calendar access is off, tap Open Settings and allow it. MyCareRoster reads your calendars on this device; nothing is copied or uploaded.

Tap an appointment, on either screen, to see it, and change or delete it, without leaving MyCareRoster. Its ••• button has Show in Calendar, which opens it in the Calendar app, and Unlink if it isn't with this provider after all. When the appointment is for one of your family members, unlinking keeps who it's for, so it moves to No provider yet on their screen.

Appointments you added elsewhere

An event that mentions the provider's surname but wasn't added through MyCareRoster is listed on their screen as a Possible match. So is an appointment with no provider yet whose title or notes name them. If it's theirs, tap its ••• button and choose Link to the provider. MyCareRoster asks who it's for (unless it already knows) and where it is, and if that changes the event's location, it asks you to confirm and adds a note of the earlier location to the event. After linking one, it offers to link other events like it.

If someone else sent you the event as an invitation, the other people invited may see the MyCareRoster link.

The sample family's appointments are samples to explore. They aren't in Calendar, and nothing is read from or written to Calendar while the sample family is shown.

9. Pasting a reminder

When a practice sends an appointment reminder, booking confirmation, change, or cancellation by text message or email, MyCareRoster can read it and update Calendar for you.

  1. In Messages, WhatsApp, Mail, or wherever it arrived, copy the whole message.
  2. On the provider's screen, tap Paste beside Reminder or confirmation text. If you're not sure which provider it's from, tap Paste Reminder on the family member's Appointments card, or tap + on the roster, then Paste Reminder.
  3. Check what MyCareRoster found, then choose what to do.

MyCareRoster reads the clipboard only when you tap Paste, and it doesn't keep the message.

If what you paste doesn't fit where you pasted it, MyCareRoster asks before doing anything. For example, a reminder that names another provider offers to use it for them, to find them, or to use it for this provider anyway; contact details pasted where a reminder was expected offer to update the contact or add a new provider.

Who it's from

When you paste from the roster or a family member's screen, MyCareRoster asks Who Is It From? and suggests providers whose name or practice is in the message. It never matches by phone number or email address. You can also choose someone in Contacts who isn't a provider yet (they're added to your Medical list), create a new contact from the message, or search your providers and contacts. If you share only some contacts, only those are searched, and Share a Contact lets you share another. When you paste on a family member's screen, MyCareRoster starts with them as the person the appointment is for. If the provider isn't in anyone's teams yet, it asks whose roster they join.

If the message doesn't say who it's with, or you don't know yet, tap Add Without a Provider under Don't Know Who It's With?. MyCareRoster asks whose appointment it is (unless you pasted on their screen, or you have one family member), then where it is, and opens Calendar's event editor with the message's date and time. The appointment appears under No provider yet on that person's screen, ready to link later. Moves, cancellations, and replies still need a provider.

Messages without punctuation sometimes run a name into the next words. If the name looks wrong, tap Correct the Name and type it; the suggestions and the Create button follow. When a message gives only a first name, such as "with Matthew", MyCareRoster lists the people with that first name for you to choose from.

Checking the details

The review shows what kind of message it is, such as a reminder, a cancellation notice, or an appointment that moved, with the date and time it found. It warns you when a date could be read two ways, or has already passed, and lets you set the date if the message doesn't give one. If the provider is in more than one family member's care teams, choose who the appointment is for.

Choose the location from the provider's addresses in Contacts, an address in the message, or Phone Consultation or Video Consultation. For a video consultation, you can add the message's video link to the event's notes after checking its address.

Updating Calendar

  • A new appointment: Add to Calendar opens Calendar's event editor, filled in.
  • A moved appointment: if there's already an appointment with the provider nearby, MyCareRoster suggests moving it instead, so Calendar doesn't keep both. Update Appointment shows the time and location now and after, for you to confirm.
  • A cancellation: Delete from Calendar finds the appointment and asks before deleting it.

Nothing changes in Calendar until you confirm.

Replying, calling, and links

MyCareRoster can't send replies. If the message says, for example, to reply Y to confirm, it shows what to reply, and you reply in the app the message came in. If it asks you to call, MyCareRoster offers to call the number, and adds a note of the call to the appointment.

Scam messages often copy appointment reminders. MyCareRoster shows a link's full address and asks before opening it or adding it to an event. Only open links you expect from that practice.

If the message gives the provider's name more fully than Contacts does, such as a middle name or a title, MyCareRoster offers Update the Name in Contacts. Only the title and names change, and a middle name the message leaves out is kept.

10. Pasting provider details

Start a new provider contact from details you've copied, instead of typing them in.

  1. Copy the provider's details from a practice webpage, an email signature, or a contact card. For a business card, open it in Camera or Photos, touch and hold the card's text, then tap Copy.
  2. Open a family member and tap +, then Add Provider, or open a team and tap Add Team Member. Under More Ways to Add, choose Paste Provider Details.
  3. Tap Paste. If the text names several people, choose the one to add. If it looks like an appointment reminder instead, MyCareRoster asks whether to open it as a reminder or add a new provider from it.
  4. Check the details. You can change the title, names, credentials, specialty, department, role, and practice, and choose which phone numbers, email addresses, addresses, and websites to include. Practices often share phone numbers and emails between providers, so include only the ones you want on this contact.
  5. Tap Continue in Contacts. Apple's contact editor opens, filled in, so you can check everything before it's saved. Tap Done.

The new contact is added to your Medical list and to the family member's roster, or to the team. MyCareRoster doesn't keep the pasted text.

When the provider is already in Contacts

If a contact with the same name exists, MyCareRoster offers it under Already in Contacts?, so you don't create a duplicate. Tap Use to add it as it is, or Update with Pasted Details to choose what to change. Adding something new starts turned on; replacing something already on the contact starts off. Nothing is removed, and nothing is saved until you confirm.

Credentials and specialty

Credentials, such as MBBS FRACGP, are saved in the contact's Suffix field. MyCareRoster keeps the specialty for the provider, and in Contacts it's saved as the contact's job title unless you enter a role.

11. Keeping details up to date

Names, credentials, job titles, phone numbers, email addresses, websites, organizations, and addresses always come from Contacts. Update a provider's card in Contacts, then pull down on the roster (or choose Refresh in its + menu) to see the change. MyCareRoster also reads Contacts whenever it starts up. Your roles, specialties, notes, teams, and primary GP choices stay as they are.

Fields Contacts may hide

MyCareRoster reads a provider's title (Prefix), middle name, credentials (Suffix), job title, and department. Contacts doesn't always show the Suffix and Job Title fields, but you can add them. Credentials in the Suffix field are what What the letters mean explains, and they keep providers sorted by surname.

  • iPhone and iPad: open the contact, tap Edit, scroll near the bottom, and tap Add Field. The list offers only the fields the card isn't already showing.
  • Mac: in Contacts, choose Contacts > Settings > Template, then use Add Field to choose a field, such as Suffix or Job Title. The template sets which fields every card shows.

When a provider's contact can't be read

Taking a contact out of your Medical list doesn't remove the provider. If their contact is deleted, or, when you share only some contacts, is no longer shared, MyCareRoster keeps their notes and settings but hides their contact details. In their teams, they're marked No longer in Contacts, or, when you share only some contacts, Deleted, or no longer shared with MyCareRoster. Tap the ••• button beside them:

  • Reconnect lets you choose their contact. Their notes, role, specialty, and teams are kept.
  • Share Again, when you share only some contacts, lets you share their contact with MyCareRoster again.
  • Remove from Team takes them out of that team. Their contact isn't deleted.

12. Private profiles

Each family member has a private profile for the details forms and clinics ask for. Open it from the family member's screen, then tap Unlock with Face ID (or Touch ID, Optic ID, or Passcode, depending on your device).

What a profile holds

  • Identity: names, including their legal name and the name they like to be called. The preferred name is their name throughout the app.
  • Credentials: government identifiers, insurance coverage, and care organization identifiers, such as a hospital record number (MRN).
  • Health: allergies, medicines, conditions, and procedures, each with a name and details.

Every field is optional except the preferred name, which is the family member's name in your roster. Add what you know now and the rest later.

Adding and changing details

Tap a section, such as Medicines. If it's empty, it opens ready to fill in. Otherwise, tap Edit, make your changes, and tap Save.

Copying into a form

Open a section and tap the copy button beside a value, then paste it into a form. Copied values stay on this device (they don't go to your other devices through Universal Clipboard) and clear from the clipboard after two minutes.

Hidden numbers

Credential numbers are hidden until you turn on Show credential values. If the profile has been open for more than five minutes, it asks for Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode again first.

Staying locked

An unlocked profile stays open for five minutes. After that, revealed numbers hide again, and MyCareRoster asks you to authenticate before you open, change, or copy anything more. Anything you're typing in an editor is kept. Numbers also hide as soon as you leave the app, and an open section is covered in the app switcher. It's locked again each time you open it.

Private profiles stay on this device unless you turn on iCloud sync (see iCloud and your other devices).

The quick card

For the moments you need them fast, you can show a family member's allergies, medicines, conditions, and procedures on their screen without Face ID. It's off unless you turn it on.

  1. Open their private profile and unlock it.
  2. Under Health, turn on Quick card without Face ID (or Touch ID, or Passcode).
  3. MyCareRoster explains what it means and asks you to confirm with Show Without Face ID.

The Quick Card then sits near the top of their screen, below their primary GP. Anyone holding your unlocked phone can see it. It's on this device only, so your other devices aren't changed, and credentials and the rest of the private profile stay locked. To hide it, tap Turn Off beside its heading, or turn the switch off in their private profile. Turning it off never asks for Face ID. The sample family doesn't have a quick card.

13. Sharing a summary

Share Summary makes a PDF of a family member's care to send to a new clinic or someone who helps, or to print.

  1. On the family member's screen, tap •••, then Share Summary.
  2. Check What's in it: their providers, with names, practices, phone numbers, emails, and suburbs. Their primary GP comes first.
  3. To add their allergies, medicines, conditions, and procedures from their private profile, turn on Include allergies, medicines, conditions, and procedures. MyCareRoster asks for Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode when it makes the PDF, every time, even if the quick card is on.
  4. Tap Make PDF, check the preview, then tap Share or Print to send it by Messages, Mail, or AirDrop, save it to Files, or print it.

A summary never includes notes, health card, insurance, or hospital numbers, or anyone else's details. It's made on your device, and MyCareRoster deletes its copy when you close the summary.

14. iCloud and your other devices

Your roster of family members, care teams, roles, specialties, notes, provider icons you've chosen, and primary GPs syncs automatically between your iPhone and iPad through your private iCloud account. It's encrypted on your device first, with a key kept in iCloud Keychain, so iCloud holds only encrypted data. Only providers you've set up are included: those in a team, chosen as a primary GP, given a role, specialty, note, or chosen icon, or reconnected to a contact. Theme and quick card settings stay on each device.

To sync, sign in to the same Apple Account on each device, with iCloud and iCloud Keychain (Passwords and Keychain) turned on. Contact details aren't copied between devices: each device reads them from its own Contacts, so your Medical list needs to be on each device too, for example through iCloud Contacts. So that your devices can match providers, the encrypted roster includes their names and organizations, and a one-way fingerprint (a hash) of their phone numbers and email addresses. See the Privacy Policy.

Setting up a new device

A new device may need a moment for iCloud Keychain to deliver the encryption key. If your teams haven't appeared, tap Sync saved care teams, or pull down on the roster. If a private profile says Waiting for iCloud Keychain, check that iCloud Keychain is on, keep the device online and unlocked for a few minutes, and tap Try Again.

Providers to reconnect

If MyCareRoster can't be sure which contact on this device matches a provider saved on another device, it lists them under Providers to reconnect. Tap one and choose the same person. Similar names are shown first, under In Your Medical List and In Your Contacts, or tap Choose in Contacts…. Confirm with Connect Same Provider. A contact from outside your Medical list is added to it when Contacts allows. Their notes, role, and teams are restored. Nothing is matched until you confirm.

If they're no longer needed, use their ••• button and choose Remove from All Teams. Their contact isn't deleted.

Syncing private profiles

Private profiles don't sync unless you choose to. To open them on your other devices, turn on Sync with iCloud in Settings, under Private Profiles, on each device. They're encrypted on the device before they're stored in iCloud.

When you turn sync off, choose Stop Syncing Only to keep the iCloud copy, or Stop Syncing and Delete from iCloud to remove it. Either way, the profiles stay on this device. Turn sync off on your other devices first, or they'll upload their copy again.

15. Free version and MyCareRoster Unlimited

The free version never expires. It includes:

  • one family member
  • two care teams
  • three providers across those teams (a provider in more than one team counts once)

Private profiles, the quick card, Share Summary, notes, appointments, pasting, themes, and encrypted iCloud sync are included free.

MyCareRoster Unlimited removes the limits on family members, care teams, and providers. It's a one-time purchase, not a subscription, and works on all your devices signed in to the same App Store account. When you reach a limit, MyCareRoster tells you which one and offers See MyCareRoster Unlimited. You can also find it in Settings, under Purchases.

If adding a provider from a family member's screen would need a new Providers team and there's no room for one, MyCareRoster asks which of their teams to add them to instead.

To restore your purchase on a new device, sign in with the same App Store account and tap Restore Purchases in Settings or on the MyCareRoster Unlimited screen.

16. Settings

Tap Settings on the roster.

  • Getting Started: Show Welcome Screen, and Explore Sample Family or Turn Off Sample Data.
  • Appearance: Theme (see Themes).
  • Private Profiles: Sync with iCloud, and How Syncing Works.
  • Contacts: whether MyCareRoster has full, limited, or no access to Contacts, Share More Contacts when you share only some, a button to change access in the Settings app, and What MyCareRoster Can See.
  • Appointments: Calendar for New Appointments, the calendar Add Appointment uses. Choose Calendar's Default or any calendar you can add events to. This choice is kept on this device only, and you can still pick another calendar in the event editor.
  • Purchases: your MyCareRoster Unlimited status, See MyCareRoster Unlimited, Restore Purchases, and Redeem Code for an offer code.
  • About: the version, Privacy Policy, Support, Rate MyCareRoster, and Send Feedback, which opens an email to us. Please don't include health details or identifiers.

Themes

In Settings, under Appearance, tap Theme. Choose Automatic to follow your device's Light or Dark setting, or Light or Dark, and one of four colours: Teal, Green, Violet, or Coral. Teal and Dark are the default.

Whatever the theme, the accent colour marks what you can tap, and amber marks what matters now, such as each person's primary GP and their next appointment. Colours get stronger when Increase Contrast is on in your device's Accessibility settings. Your choice stays on this device.

17. Deleting your information

Deleting MyCareRoster from a device removes what it stored on that device. Copies in iCloud remain until you remove them.

MyCareRoster's Diagnostics section has tools for this. To show it, open Settings and tap the version number seven times. To hide it again, tap the lock icon beside the version.

  • Delete from iCloud removes the encrypted iCloud copy of your roster, your private profiles, or both. This device keeps its own copy and stops syncing it until you tap Resume iCloud Uploads or hide Diagnostics.
  • Delete Everything permanently deletes every family member, team, provider setting, note, and private profile from iCloud and this device, along with the encryption keys, and turns off quick cards and resets the theme on this device. Delete MyCareRoster from your other devices first, or they'll upload their copy again.

Both ask for Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode. Neither changes your contacts, which stay in Contacts, or your appointments, which stay in Calendar.

Diagnostics also has Support Info, which we may ask you to copy into an email if you need help. See Support.
